## 2. 🔴 The seat cap — the finding that gates everything else

### The game cannot seat eight. Confirmed two ways.

**Mechanism 1 — reading the code.** `MAX_PLAYERS.ENSEMBLE = 6`
(`server/utils/constants.ts:11`), enforced at `room.handler.ts:186`. Not overridden anywhere and
there is no env knob. The 7th joiner is **not rejected** — they are seated as `SPECTATOR`
(`room.handler.ts:203`). `game.helpers.ts:117-125` then builds the trait list from **PLAYER-role
selections only**, so six is also the ceiling on what reaches the model.

**Mechanism 2 — a live room.** Eight real socket clients driven through a complete ENSEMBLE round
against the real handlers, reading the prompt out of the mock Anthropic endpoint:

```
8 joiners → 6 PLAYER, 2 SPECTATOR, 0 rejected      (roster: 9 people, incl. host)
CRITICAL: You have 6 characters...                 (what the model actually received)
```

**The room holds nine bodies; the scene holds six parts.** The host is role `HOST`, so they run the
teleprompter without consuming a seat or contributing a trait.

### 🔴 The live UI advertises a cap the server does not honour

`app/host/components/HostLobby.tsx:338` renders `Max {gameMode === 'HEAD_TO_HEAD' ? 2 : 8}`, while
the mode selector 165 lines below says *"3-6 performers + host"*. **Both strings are in the bundle
serving right now** — I re-confirmed after the deploy.

The failure mode is exactly your playtest: you read "Max 8", invite eight people, and guests seven
and eight are silently seated as spectators.

**Left unfixed on purpose.** Which of the two numbers is wrong *is* the cap decision.

**If eight is load-bearing because it is your playtest, raise the cap. If six is the better scene,
change the freeze wording instead.** Same decision from opposite ends; only you can pick.

## 3. The URL bug the rename uncovered — the real content of the work

The record said `metadataBase` "falls back to the dead domain" and the fix was "one line plus a
restart". **Both were wrong**, and the truth was worse:

`next.config.js` declared `NEXT_PUBLIC_APP_URL: process.env.NEXT_PUBLIC_APP_URL ||
'http://localhost:3000'` in its `env` block, which Next inlines at **build** time. Every
`BASE_URL || APP_URL || <domain>` chain in the app stopped at the **second** term. The dead domain
everyone worried about was unreachable dead code.

What the live site was serving, read from the response rather than the source:

```
og:image   →  http://localhost:3000/opengraph-image
robots.txt →  Sitemap: http://localhost:3000/sitemap.xml
sitemap.xml → <loc>http://localhost:3000</loc>
```

**And because these are `NEXT_PUBLIC_*`, they bake into the bundle** — setting the variable in
`/etc/plotslop/env` and restarting would have done nothing. It needed a rebuild.

Replaced with `lib/siteUrl.ts`, one `NODE_ENV`-aware constant, and the `next.config.js` default
deleted. **Now verified live** — see §4.

### Four dead domains, not one

| Domain | Where | Why it mattered |
|---|---|---|
| **`plottwists.com`** | `HostLobby.tsx:253,310` | **Resolves to `156.254.10.135`** — a live host that is not yours (two independent resolvers; HTTP 444). It was the **join instruction on the host's screen**: *"plottwists.com/join → CODE"*. At a party that is the sentence people read and type. |
| `plottwists.app` | `robots.ts`, `sitemap.ts`, both replay files, `user.handler.ts`, `JsonLd.tsx` | No DNS. Was the SEO canonical and share-URL base. |
| `plottwists.live` | `poster-story`, `CharacterCardShare`, `DirectorsReview`, `ReferralCard` | No DNS. Watermarked into generated share images. |

**The generalisable lesson:** a grep for the name you renamed *from* will not find the names you
never knew you had.

game mechanic** — `PlotTwistVoting`, `plotTwistTimeouts`, `PLOT_TWIST_VOTING_DURATION`. A blind
